# Adversarial investor review

**Scope:** current `proposal.source.html` and its static source sections.  
**Method:** independent institutional-investor, angel-investor and Australian operating-business reader tests, followed by an editorial synthesis.  
**Bottom line:** the study is a strong, unusually candid pre-feasibility and validation case. It is **not yet** a financeable plant proposal or equity offer. The credible current ask is a tightly governed A$380k validation programme.

## Investment decisions today

| Reader | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional / IC | Decline plant or commercial-equity capital; consider staged validation only | Feed, buyer, technical, permit and financial proof are still future gates. |
| Angel | Pass on a cheque; meet only with a credible operating team and defined deal | No founder/team, legal entity, security or valuation is stated. |
| Australian operator | Do not order a pilot plant | Throughput, site, approvals, offtake and all-in economics are not yet proved. |

## What already works

- **The core insight is clear.** Sheets 01–03 explain that the issue is not merely metal recovery: transport and mixed fractions destroy value before a buyer sees the product.
- **The study is honest in the main body.** Sheets 15, 51–56 and 61 distinguish a model or test from a result, preserve a mechanical benchmark and define stop gates.
- **The staged capital logic is appropriate.** Sheets 48, 49 and 61 separate validation from a later plant decision.
- **The process record is transparent.** Sheets 20 and 62–85 show alternatives, dead ends, dependencies and a fallback rather than pretending the selected mechanism is inevitable.

## Deal-breaking gaps

### 1. No commercial proof

**Evidence:** Sheets 02, 39, 40, 45, 54 and 61 all list signed feed, buyer specification, accepted lots and settlement as evidence still to be obtained.

There is no named customer, feed agreement, price schedule, buyer letter, trial order, assay, settlement sheet or contracted collection lane. The A$18/panel service fee and A$12/panel materials value on Sheet 45 are therefore model inputs, not revenue evidence.

**Correction now in source:** `39a-commercial-proof.html` is a one-page commercial proof scorecard. It states that no feed contract or buyer settlement term is secured, then lists the evidence, owner-facing next action and residuals check required before the model becomes underwritable.

### 2. The first-plant underwriting case is inconsistent

**Evidence:** Sheet 03, Sheet 47 and the base case use 45,000 panels/year; Sheet 49 uses 90,000 panels/year only for the upside option. Earlier drafts used a 100,000-panel pilot figure, which is no longer in the source. The remaining question is whether the 45,000-panel screening basis survives vendor, site and customer diligence.

An investor cannot assess permits, staffing, equipment, working capital or feed contracts against three different plants.

**Current correction:** 45,000 accepted panels/year is now the single first-plant screening basis. The 90,000-panel case is labelled as an expansion option; national 100kt/y figures remain market context only. The remaining underwriting work is to replace this screening basis with a quoted site, crew, panel mix, customer lane, CAPEX, OPEX and minimum contracted feed.

### 3. Economics do not clear a risk-adjusted return hurdle

**Evidence:** Sheets 45 and 49 model A$16.20 per-panel contribution, A$244k site EBITDA and an 8.2-year simple payback on A$2.0m CAPEX. Sheet 45 excludes fixed site costs; Sheet 49 excludes central overhead, working capital, tax, financing, ramp-up, technical failure and sustaining CAPEX.

This is a useful screening model but cannot support plant capital. The base case relies on uncontracted revenue, while the attractive upside is driven by a modelled mobile-freight saving.

**Required correction:** add one reconciled project P&L and cash-flow model that allocates each cost once, includes contingency, working capital, ramp, insurance, closure/residuals and financing, and states an explicit investment hurdle (IRR/NPV or target payback). Do not lead with EBITDA alone.

### 4. The technical claim is still an experiment, and IP is not a moat

**Evidence:** Sheets 20, 23–28, 42–43 and 73–76 show that controlled release remains unvalidated, while mechanical treatment is the practical benchmark and relevant patent territory is crowded.

The report’s technical honesty is a strength. But the narrative sometimes lets the selected process feel more settled than the evidence permits.

**Required correction:** describe the thesis as a **comparative experiment**: selected release must beat mechanical preparation on delivered margin, product acceptance and safety across real panel cohorts. Do not present a protectable technology position until FTO, ownership and repeatability are evidenced.

### 5. Team, entity and security are missing

**Evidence:** the cover and contact point identify d/rksci, but no section states the legal entity, founder, operating team, governance, IP ownership, security, valuation or investor rights.

This alone prevents an angel or institutional investor from underwriting an equity investment.

**Correction now in source:** `61a-sponsor-and-terms.html` makes the missing entity, team, instrument, ownership, reporting and decision fields explicit. It remains an open diligence requirement; the page does not invent a sponsor structure or imply that securities are being offered.

## Editorial and structure review

### The strongest current story

The opening sequence—**Blue Sky Mine → Value Gap → Minimum Viable Opportunity → Waste Wave**—works. It makes the counter-intuitive point that preserving quality can matter more than extracting every gram of metal.

### Where it loses an investor

1. The report moves into broad technical exploration before stating the actual investment instrument, sponsor and hard commercial evidence.
2. The 48-concept lineage is excellent due diligence but can read as research abundance rather than focused execution. It belongs behind the selected experiment, not in front of the investor decision.
3. Terms such as *one-glass release*, *slimes*, *NIR*, *FTO*, *SOM* and *crack front* are explainable, but not all are explained at first use in the investor path.
4. Some appendices retain precise numbers and engineering claims which may look like company performance, despite caveats in nearby copy. That creates a credibility leak between the main study’s disciplined language and the technical archive.
5. The visual/reporting quality signals a serious study, but its density asks a cheque-writer to work too hard to discover the ask, the team, the downside and the proof plan.

## Recommended investor-path rewrite

Keep the research archive intact. Create a **12–16 sheet investor path** within the report, then direct technical readers to the full record.

1. Cover and one-sentence promise.
2. The problem: panels are bulky, mixed and costly to move.
3. The insight: quality preservation can beat indiscriminate crushing.
4. What exists today: mechanical baseline and incumbent alternatives.
5. The chosen experiment: two release hypotheses versus the baseline.
6. The customer proposition: exact first customer, first lane and product.
7. The first-plant design basis: one capacity, one crew, one site basis.
8. Unit economics: base/downside only, with proof status beside every input.
9. Why this can win: measurable delivered-margin or traceability advantage.
10. Risks and hard stop rules.
11. The A$380k validation plan: outputs, owners, dates and tranches.
12. Sponsor, transaction terms and explicit decision requested.

Then retain the present process study, full lineage, prior-art analysis, comparison charts, evidence register and detailed appendices as the evidence room.

## Evidence threshold before a plant decision

1. Signed or price-bearing feed agreements sufficient for the selected first-plant capacity.
2. Two representative panel cohorts run through the baseline and selected alternatives with independent mass balance, energy, water, labour, emissions, failure and cycle-time distributions.
3. Multiple buyer-accepted commercial lots with assay, impurity, moisture, settlement, freight and payment evidence.
4. Site-specific permit path, residue classification/outlet, insurance and an approval schedule.
5. Reconciled vendor quotes, line balance, labour plan, working-capital/ramp/contingency model and investment hurdle.
6. Patent counsel claim chart/FTO plus clear ownership of foreground IP and data.
7. Named team, entity and financing instrument.

## Recommended decision language

> **Do not fund a recycling plant yet. Fund a bounded validation programme only if it can secure commercial evidence before equipment is ordered.** The work must prove that a selected process creates a buyer-accepted product and a better delivered margin than mechanical preparation, from contracted Australian feed.

This review deliberately separates the quality of the research from the maturity of the business. The research clears the threshold for disciplined validation. The business has not yet cleared the threshold for plant CAPEX or priced equity.
